Mitacs Research Project: AI/ML Development for Measurement-Based Mental Health Care

A Mitacs project, in collaboration with Kelello Health and CAMH, building models and data pipelines for clinical assessment, patient monitoring, and decision support.

Approach and application

This Mitacs project develops AI and machine learning solutions to support measurement-based care in mental health applications. In collaboration with Kelello Health and CAMH, the project aims to build intelligent models and data-driven pipelines for clinical assessment, patient monitoring, and decision support.

By integrating large language models, multimodal learning, and robust predictive analytics, the work explores how heterogeneous healthcare data, including structured clinical records, patient-reported outcomes, and text-based information, can be transformed into actionable insights. The project emphasizes fairness, reliability, and real-world deployment.

Intelligent Flight Training Scheduling and Cancellation Recovery

With Diamond Flight Centre London, a scheduling system that coordinates students, instructors, aircraft, and simulators, and finds a feasible replacement when a lesson is cancelled.

Approach and application

At Diamond Flight Centre London (DFC), based at London International Airport, a lesson cancelled because of student unavailability, adverse weather, or an aircraft fault can leave an aircraft, an instructor, or a simulator unused. Recovering that slot requires an eligible student, a qualified instructor, and a suitable aircraft or simulator to be available together, and the feasible options depend on why the lesson was cancelled.

The project is building a scheduling system that brings student, instructor, aircraft, and simulator availability into one schedule and checks each booking against training stage, instructor qualifications, maintenance, daylight, and weather. When a lesson is cancelled, the system identifies feasible replacements, contacts eligible students, confirms the new booking, and updates the schedule. When weather prevents flying, it can propose a suitable simulator lesson so that training continues.

Building on this foundation, the research formulates pilot training scheduling as optimization under uncertainty and investigates learning-based predictions of cancellations, weather disruptions, and resource availability. The resulting scheduling and recovery framework will be evaluated with DFC against rule-based replacement and deterministic optimization, measuring recovered training hours, aircraft and simulator utilization, and recovery time. The project is supported by Mitacs and NSERC.

Wildfire Early Detection – UAV-Satellite Forecasting System

UAVs collect localized multimodal data to complement satellite observations, with lightweight models on board and heavier processing at a control center.

Approach and application

Many fire-prone areas, especially remote boreal zones, lack adequate ground-based sensing or rapid-response systems. This project combines unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) with satellite and environmental data. UAVs act as mobile surveillance units that reach regions where human deployment is impractical and autonomously collect localized multimodal data.

To accommodate limited onboard resources, a collaborative AI deployment strategy runs lightweight model components on the UAVs while more intensive processing is handled by the control center. A dynamic database at the control center supports continuous model updating as wildfire patterns evolve, with the aim of supporting early intervention and reducing response times.

Lightweight RIS-assisted Localization for Indoor Intelligent Wireless Systems

A lightweight fingerprint-based framework that uses a small number of configurable RIS patterns to locate devices indoors, where GPS is unavailable.

Approach and application

Accurate indoor localization is increasingly important for intelligent wireless systems, as smart environments, context-aware services, and autonomous indoor operations rely on reliable position information. Indoor localization remains challenging due to severe multipath propagation, blockage, dynamic obstacles, and the absence of GPS coverage.

This project investigates how reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) can enable practical and efficient indoor localization. Instead of relying on explicit path separation or high-complexity geometric parameter estimation, it adopts a lightweight fingerprint-based framework that directly exploits location-dependent wireless responses under a small number of configurable RIS patterns.

The research focuses on improving fingerprint discriminability among nearby locations and reducing offline radio-map construction cost through lightweight sampling and completion methods.

RIS-Assisted Indoor Wireless Localization: Algorithm Design and Experimental Validation

Localization techniques that combine directional antennas, RIS-enabled signal manipulation, and geometry, validated on a software-defined radio testbed with RIS hardware and USRP platforms.

Approach and application

This project explores efficient indoor wireless localization using reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs). It investigates localization techniques that exploit directional antenna characteristics, RIS-enabled signal manipulation, and geometric relationships to estimate user positions.

A software-defined radio (SDR) testbed integrating RIS hardware, directional antennas, and USRP platforms is being developed to investigate the feasibility, accuracy, and robustness of the proposed localization techniques in practical indoor wireless environments.

Smart Transportation – UAV-Assisted Intersection Safety System

With Beware Company: UAVs as aerial surveillance nodes, roadside units as edge servers, and semantic transmission of only task-critical information.

Approach and application

Urban intersections are high-risk zones, particularly with mixed mobility such as bicycles, scooters, and lightweight vehicles. In partnership with Beware Company, the project is developing a 3D smart transportation system based on UAV-assisted surveillance.

UAVs create a real-time view of intersection activity and collaborate with roadside traffic units acting as edge servers for low-latency processing. A semantic transmission method shares only task-critical information instead of raw streams. When potential collision risks are detected, alerts are sent to users’ smartphones.
